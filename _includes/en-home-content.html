Research Profile

Dr. Honglin Zhu is an Associate Professor in the Department of Rheumatology and Immunology at Xiangya Hospital, Central South University. Her research focuses on refractory autoimmune diseases, particularly systemic sclerosis and idiopathic inflammatory myopathies.

Dr. Zhu’s research integrates single-cell transcriptomics, spatial transcriptomics, and bulk multi-omics profiling — including transcriptomics, epigenomics, and metabolomics — with artificial intelligence models and comprehensive clinical phenotyping. Through this multidisciplinary approach, her work aims to characterize the immune microenvironment of autoimmune diseases, identify pathogenic cell populations, clarify disease mechanisms, and translate molecular findings into precision classification systems, prognostic biomarkers, and therapeutic targets.

Research Focus Areas

1. Systemic Sclerosis

Dr. Zhu’s group investigates pathogenic fibroblast and macrophage subtypes associated with skin fibrosis, interstitial lung disease progression, and long-term prognosis in systemic sclerosis. Her research aims to define cellular and molecular signatures that support disease stratification, outcome prediction, and targeted therapeutic development (J Invest Dermatol. 2025, 2024; J Autoimmun. 2024).

2. Idiopathic Inflammatory Myopathies

Her group studies the immune microenvironment of muscle injury in idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, with a particular focus on disease-driving immune and stromal cell populations. This work aims to develop molecularly informed strategies for precision subtype classification and clinical outcome prediction (Ann Rheum Dis. 2026, 2024; J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle. 2025).

3. Refractory Autoimmune Disease and Immunometabolism

Dr. Zhu is particularly interested in refractory forms of systemic sclerosis and idiopathic inflammatory myopathies. By linking inflammation, immunometabolic reprogramming, multi-omics signatures, and longitudinal clinical outcomes, her research seeks to uncover mechanisms of treatment resistance and identify novel therapeutic opportunities (Arthritis Rheumatol. 2026; Metabolism. 2023; J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle. 2022).

Research Vision

Dr. Zhu’s long-term vision is to establish a clinically integrated, multi-omics framework for refractory autoimmune diseases. By combining advanced molecular profiling, computational modeling, and longitudinal clinical data, her research aims to advance precision medicine in rheumatology and improve outcomes for patients with systemic sclerosis and idiopathic inflammatory myopathies.
